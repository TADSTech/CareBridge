Slide 02 · The Problem

Language and health literacy block care — even when care is present.

Inclusion is not putting a hospital where people can reach it. It is making the healthcare experience understandable once they get there.

Second-language intake

Patients cannot accurately describe symptoms, history, or pain in a language that is not theirs.

Opaque instructions

A patient may “read” English and still not understand a prescription, discharge note, or consent form.

Downstream risk

Miscommunication drives misdiagnosis risk, wasted clinic time, poor adherence, and loss of trust.

Without a shared layer
  • Symptoms filtered through a second language
  • Clinician note ≠ patient’s own words
  • Instructions leave the room unclear
  • Trust and follow-up erode
→
With CareBridge
  • Patient speaks naturally in their language
  • Structured clinical summary for the clinician
  • Response returned as simple text + speech
  • Both sides leave aligned

Slide 06 · Scope

What CareBridge is — and is not.

A hard line around scope, on purpose. Communication infrastructure only — clinical judgment stays with the healthcare worker.

✕
Not replacing doctors

CareBridge never sits between clinician judgment and the patient as a substitute provider.

✕
Not diagnosing patients

No diagnostic claims. Structured summaries inform the clinician; they do not replace examination.

✕
Not a consumer paywall

We do not ask sick people to pay for access to their own care conversation.

We solve this

Making sure the patient can be heard — and can understand the person responsible for their care.

Leave with clear instructions
